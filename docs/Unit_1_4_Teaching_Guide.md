# Unit 1.4 — The Incarnation

Companion to **Incarnation_Lecture.html**, a 20-slide standalone lecture based on the supplied **Unit 1.4.docx**. Use an NRSV-CE Bible for the seven read-aloud selections; the slides contain references and listening prompts rather than full passages.

## Intended understanding

Students should explain the Incarnation and hypostatic union, identify four purposes of the Son’s coming, distinguish Christ’s complete divinity and humanity in one Person, correct the source’s five early errors, and connect this teaching with salvation and daily life. Assess understanding and reasoned application rather than personal religious assent.

## Sequence and connections

Allow approximately 60–70 minutes. A two-part lesson can pause after slide 10.

1. **Slides 1–5, about 15 minutes:** retrieve Lessons 1.1–1.3; define the Incarnation through John’s prologue, person/nature language and the Nativity.
2. **Slides 6–10, about 20 minutes:** use the four-purpose overview as a map, then read and discuss one passage for each purpose.
3. **Slides 11–13, about 10 minutes:** develop complete humanity, knowledge and will. Reveal a careful explanation of how God the Son suffers in his humanity.
4. **Slides 14–17, about 12 minutes:** group the five errors by the truth at stake. Explain Mary’s title and the councils; use the correction exercise to check understanding.
5. **Slides 18–20, about 10 minutes:** read Hebrews, apply dignity and solidarity, and complete the exit ticket.

Lesson **1.1** considers the source of existence. Lesson **1.2** explains created action within dependence on God. Lesson **1.3** confesses the personal, triune God of saving love. Lesson **1.4** asks how the eternal Son truly enters human history. A First Cause argument alone does not establish the Incarnation; this lesson presents the Christian claim received through revelation.

Lesson **1.5** develops the Paschal Mystery that this lesson prepares students to understand. Lesson **1.6** develops Baptism into new life; **1.7** develops Christ’s priestly role. Later lessons can retrieve dignity, embodied life, grace, freedom and charity.

## Activities and model responses

- **Slide 13:** “God suffered” is carefully explained as God the Son truly suffering in his human nature. The divine nature does not become mortal; the Father and Spirit are not the Persons who become incarnate.
- **Slide 17:** Jesus is not promoted into divinity at Baptism, and his humanity does not become irrelevant. The eternal Son assumes complete humanity from conception and remains fully divine and human.
- **Slide 19:** choose healing, solidarity, example or belonging, then explain a concrete action such as caring for a vulnerable person or offering responsible service. A hypothetical or third-person reflection is acceptable.
- **Slide 20:** a strong exit response uses precise person/nature language, names the four purposes, explains what a doctrinal correction protects, and connects Scripture with an earlier lesson and an action today.

## Teaching clarifications

The lecture retains the note’s central Catholic teaching while qualifying illustrations and generalizations:

- **Trinity and Incarnation:** three divine Persons share one divine nature. The Son alone becomes incarnate, in one Person with two complete natures. Avoid percentages, a hybrid nature, two personal subjects, or a fourth Person.
- **Conception and birth:** the Son assumes humanity from conception. Christmas celebrates his birth, not the beginning of his divine existence. Baptism reveals who he is; it does not make him divine.
- **Complete humanity:** real body, rational soul, intellect, will and emotion belong to the humanity assumed. Without sin does not mean less human. Full human experience does not mean living every possible individual biography.
- **Human knowledge and willing:** growth and learning are real, while divine knowledge remains unlimited. The Catechism also describes Christ’s unique human knowledge of the Father and mission. Gethsemane’s real anguish and natural aversion to death are compatible with freely obedient human willing; absence of sin does not eliminate distress.
- **One divine saving will:** Father, Son and Spirit do not have opposed divine plans. Christ freely offers himself; avoid an unwilling Father persuaded by Jesus or a Father coercing a reluctant divine Son.
- **Predicating suffering:** the Person who suffers is God the Son, according to his humanity. Do not transfer mortality to the divine nature or imply that the Trinity dissolves at the Cross.
- **Theotokos:** Mother of God identifies the Person Mary bears in humanity. It does not make her the origin of divinity or the mother of the Father.
- **Historical terminology:** the short definitions identify doctrinal errors, not complete histories. Specify the Eutychian loss of full humanity rather than applying “Monophysite” indiscriminately to Oriental Orthodox Christians. Catholic–Coptic and Catholic–Assyrian declarations provide important context for respectful accuracy.
- **Salvation and freedom:** explain God’s chosen saving way without asserting that God lacked any conceivable alternative. Grace and imitation belong together; Christ is more than a role model.
- **Participation:** sharing divine life by grace does not turn creatures into gods by nature or give them Christ’s hypostatic union. Our adoption differs from the Son’s eternal sonship.
- **Spirit and dignity:** the Spirit was active before the Incarnation; Pentecost brings a new fullness. Human dignity precedes Christ’s birth and is further illuminated by the Incarnation. The material world is not evil in itself.
- **Solidarity:** Christ’s suffering supports compassionate care and hope; it does not make harm good, guarantee immediate relief or require remaining in abuse. The cry of abandonment belongs with Psalm 22, not a division of the Trinity.
- **Enduring union:** the risen and ascended Christ remains truly divine and truly human. Humanity is not a disguise discarded after the saving events.
