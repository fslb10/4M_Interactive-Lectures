Essential teaching clarifications

Baptism is an effective sign of grace, not water acting by natural power. Original sin is not personal wrongdoing by an infant. Forgiveness does not remove every consequence of the Fall; weakness and temptation remain. Baptismal adoption is a gift of communion; every person already has dignity. A valid Baptism is not repeated, but its permanent character does not guarantee final salvation regardless of later choices. Baptism is necessary for those who know its call and can receive it; God can save beyond sacramental access. Desire, blood and hope for unbaptized children do not create extra sacraments. Do not infer validity from denomination alone or teach the Latin formula as the Church’s only valid liturgical form. Shared priesthood does not confer ordination. The note’s alleged exceptions for receiving other sacraments before Baptism are corrected in the full notes.

Teaching priorities and assessment

Teach the stated question, the complete spoken core where supplied, and the seven full recordings. Offer the case before its model response; assess accurate explanation and a reasoned action, not personal faith or disclosure. Short core assessments do not require optional council dates, additional readings or liturgical details. The full version permits discussion and deeper connections. Suggested full pacing: 5 minutes retrieval, 40 minutes teaching and readings, 10 minutes application, 5 minutes exit/bridge; allow up to 10 extra minutes for passage discussions. Short lessons include two 30-second responses; lengthen them when the class needs more time.

Slide 4 · Matthew 28:18–20

Context

  • The risen Jesus addresses the eleven disciples on a mountain in Galilee at the end of Matthew’s Gospel.
  • After the Resurrection, he sends them beyond their earlier mission to make disciples among all nations.
  • The reading joins baptizing in the Trinitarian name, teaching obedience and Jesus’ promise of continuing presence.

Meaning

  • Baptism belongs to Christ’s commission; the Church receives its mission from him.
  • This slide connects Baptism to the Trinity and to a continuing life of learning, rather than an isolated ceremony.
  • Christian witness should invite a free response and respect the dignity of those who hear it.

Discuss: Why do baptizing and teaching belong together?

Presenter notes and teaching script

Core scripts appear first in short lessons. Additional notes give depth for discussion; optional material is outside the short core estimate. Students can explain a perspective without disclosing personal beliefs or experiences.

1. One true Baptism

Begin with the question: How does the salvation accomplished by Jesus reach a person today? Retrieve the final sacramental connection from Lesson 1.5 without reteaching the whole Paschal Mystery.

Seven core readings occur on slides 4, 5, 7, 8, 13, 17 and 18. The other Scripture references are recall or optional extensions, not additional required readings.

Learning goals: define Baptism; connect it to Christ’s death and Resurrection; explain its necessity alongside God’s mercy; identify its implications for worship, choices and service.

Batch 3: seven original complete recordings, narrator and synchronized captions retained. Context and Meaning pause audio and preserve position; close, then Play to resume. Plan 60–70 minutes: 5 retrieval, 40 explanation and readings, 10 application, 5 exit/bridge, with up to 10 extra minutes of discussion.

Catechism: the grace of Baptism

2. Why this question comes next

The earlier lesson titles are taken from the Unit 1 Resource Pack: 1.1 The Existence of God; 1.2 Causation Theory; 1.3 The Nature of God; 1.4 The Incarnation; 1.5 The Paschal Mystery.

Ask for one remembered idea from each lesson. Keep this to a brief bridge rather than five separate reviews.

The Incarnation helps explain a sacramental worldview: material creation can serve God’s saving purpose. The claim about baptismal grace rests on Christ and the Church’s sacramental faith, not on a scientific property of water.

The Trinity connection returns in the baptismal formula; the Paschal Mystery connection is developed through Romans 6.

3. A sacrament of new birth

Define sacrament in familiar language: an effective sign of grace instituted by Christ and entrusted to the Church. Do not reduce Baptism to either a naming ceremony or a public statement of beliefs.

Baptism is the gateway to the other sacraments. Baptism, Confirmation and Eucharist together form Christian initiation.

The note’s “new birth” and “gateway” images explain different aspects of the same gift: a new life begins, and a path of worship and growth opens.

4. Baptize in the name of the Trinity

Read Matthew 28:18–20 aloud from the NRSV-CE. Ask students to identify make disciples, baptize and teach.

Connect to Lesson 1.3: Father, Son and Holy Spirit are three divine Persons, one God. Avoid describing the Persons as three parts of God or three gods.

Connect to Lesson 1.4: Jesus is the eternal Son made human. Our adoption in Christ is a gift of grace; we do not become divine by nature.

The mission extends to all nations. Christian witness should respect freedom and human dignity.

Catechism: the baptismal celebration

5. United to Christ’s death and rising

Read Romans 6:3–5 slowly. Ask students to hear the repeated connection between Christ’s experience and the baptized person’s new life.

Explain sacramental participation: Jesus does not die again at each Baptism. His once-for-all saving work reaches us through the sacrament.

Water can express both death and life. Immersion makes the burial-and-rising imagery especially vivid; pouring is also a legitimate form of the rite.

The future hope of bodily resurrection remains. Baptism gives the beginning of new life now and points toward its completion with God.

6. Cleansed from sin

Use the note’s cleansing image, but avoid suggesting that a child has committed personal wrongdoing. Original sin is a condition inherited with fallen human nature.

For an adult, Baptism also forgives personal sins. CCC 1263 includes all punishment for sin.

Correct an overstatement in the note: Baptism does not remove every consequence of the Fall. Illness, death, weakness and concupiscence remain (CCC 1264).

The continuing struggle explains why the baptized still need prayer, moral growth, community and Reconciliation.

Catechism: the grace of Baptism

7. New life through the Holy Spirit

Read Titus 3:4–7. Focus on mercy, renewal by the Holy Spirit and the hope of eternal life.

Connect adoption to the Trinity: Christ is Son by nature; we become God’s children in him by grace. We remain human persons.

The note names the theological virtues and gifts of the Spirit. Explain that these gifts call for growth, rather than appearing instantly as mature habits.

Optional extension: 2 Corinthians 5:17 or Galatians 3:26–28. Use only if more explanation of new creation or adoption is needed.

Required clarification: baptismal adoption is a further gift of communion, not the source of human dignity. Human dignity belongs to every person.

Catechism: the grace of Baptism

8. One Baptism, one body

Read Ephesians 4:1–6. Do not isolate verse 5 from the humility, patience and peace in the surrounding passage.

Optional extension: 1 Corinthians 12:12–13 connects Baptism with membership in one body.

A valid Baptism is not repeated when someone enters full communion with the Catholic Church. Reception and completion of initiation depend on the person’s circumstances.

Water, the Trinitarian form and the intention to do what the Church does matter. Do not assume validity solely from a denomination’s name, or invalidity solely from a different explanation of baptismal grace. Concrete doubtful cases are examined by the Church.

Membership brings responsibilities and spiritual rights: support through God’s word, the sacraments with proper preparation, and the life of the community.

Catechism: the grace of Baptism

9. Baptized once

Indelible means it cannot be erased. This is a spiritual reality, not a visible mark or a change in personality that everyone will notice.

The character remains even if someone rejects faith or sins. CCC 1272 also states that sin can obstruct the fruits of salvation.

For a baptized Catholic who returns after serious sin, the path includes conversion and Reconciliation, not another Baptism.

A conditional Baptism concerns a serious unresolved doubt about whether a valid Baptism occurred; it is not a routine repetition of a known valid sacrament.

Code of Canon Law: Baptism and sacramental life

10. Water, words and intention

The baptismal words shown here are a liturgical formula, not a printed Bible passage. Matthew 28:19 gives the Trinitarian mandate. CCC 1240 also records a valid Eastern liturgical form, “The servant of God … is baptized …”; do not present Latin wording as the only wording used throughout the Church.

The usual ministers are bishops and priests, and in the Latin Church deacons. In necessity, anyone, even an unbaptized person, can baptize with the required intention, water and Trinitarian form (CCC 1256).

The note mentions sprinkling; teach immersion or pouring as the normal Catholic forms rather than suggesting a merely symbolic sprinkle is sufficient.

The sacrament’s efficacy comes from Christ, not the holiness or full theological understanding of the minister. This is the point behind the note’s discussion of Augustine and the Donatists.

11. From sign to response

Invite students to connect each sign to an effect already learned. Avoid reteaching the four gifts in full.

Chrism is perfumed oil consecrated by the bishop. Distinguish the post-baptismal anointing from the oil of catechumens used in preparation.

The sign of the cross, proclamation of Scripture, renunciation of sin, profession of faith and blessing of water give the rite its wider context.

Parents, godparents and the assembled Church express responsibility for nurturing faith. Their role continues beyond the ceremony.

Rite symbols retained from the teacher note: chrism signifies the Spirit and participation in Christ’s mission; the white garment signifies putting on Christ; the candle signifies Christ’s light. These explain the gift rather than replacing the essential water and Trinitarian form.

Catechism: the baptismal celebration

12. A gift for children and adults

A catechumen is an unbaptized person preparing for Christian initiation. An already validly baptized person preparing to become Catholic is not awaiting a second Baptism.

Faith is necessary, but the Church does not require a fully mature faith before Baptism. Children and adults both need ongoing formation.

Godparents should be firm believers ready to help. For a Catholic sponsor, being baptized alone is not the whole set of eligibility requirements; the parish checks suitability.

Optional Acts 2:38–42 shows repentance, Baptism and the continuing life of the community. Household Baptism texts alone do not explicitly establish the ages of every member.

Expanded comparison retained: children need new life in Christ; parents and godparents profess faith and promise support, and children learn to live the gift personally. Adults prepare through faith, conversion and learning, join the Church’s prayer and life, and continue formation after the celebration.

Code of Canon Law: Baptism and sacramental life

13. Why is Baptism necessary for salvation?

Read John 3:1–5. Nicodemus initially thinks of physical birth; explain the Catholic understanding of water and Spirit as baptismal rebirth.

Make the causal connection explicit: humanity needs redemption; Christ accomplishes it; Baptism unites us to him and gives forgiveness and new life.

CCC 1257 states the necessity for those who have heard the Gospel and can ask for the sacrament. The Church therefore baptizes and teaches rather than treating Baptism as dispensable.

Do not turn this into a claim that the ritual guarantees final salvation regardless of later choices, or that God cannot save someone who never had access to it. The next slide addresses God’s mercy.

Catechism: Baptism and salvation

14. God’s grace is not confined

Explicit desire: a catechumen who dies before Baptism, with desire for it, repentance and charity (CCC 1259).

Those who do not know the Gospel but sincerely seek truth and do God’s will as they understand it can be saved by grace; the Church understands that they would have desired Baptism had they known its necessity (CCC 1260). This is not salvation earned by being “nice.”

Baptism of blood means death for the faith before sacramental Baptism. Desire and blood bring baptismal fruits without being additional sacraments (CCC 1258).

For children who die without Baptism, teach the Church’s hope in God’s mercy (CCC 1261), rather than certainty of damnation. The note’s mention of limbo should not be taught as a defined doctrine.

Keep tone compassionate. This is not a classroom exercise in judging the eternal destiny of particular people.

Catechism: Baptism and salvation

15. Permanent character, ongoing growth

Ask students to commit to an explanation before revealing the answer. Listen for a distinction between the indelible character and the fruits of grace.

Concupiscence remains; temptation is not itself sin. Serious sin can rupture communion with God even though the baptismal mark remains.

For a baptized Catholic, sacramental Reconciliation is the ordinary path of restoration after grave sin. Baptism is not repeated.

A good answer avoids both mistakes: “Baptism stopped working” and “later choices no longer matter.”

Catechism: the grace of Baptism

16. New life needs nourishment

Baptism opens sacramental life; it does not make every other sacrament automatically appropriate without preparation or proper disposition.

The order and timing of Confirmation and Eucharist differ between Eastern and Latin practice and between pastoral settings. This diagram shows their relationship, not a universal age sequence.

Correct a misleading exception in the note: an unbaptized person cannot validly receive another sacrament. A valid marriage involving an unbaptized person is not thereby a sacramental marriage.

Reconciliation heals post-baptismal sin; Matrimony and Holy Orders direct particular vocations toward service. All Christian states of life are called to holiness.

Sunday Mass, worthy reception of Communion, prayer, Scripture and community sustain growth. Holy water and a baptismal anniversary can help Christians remember their Baptism.

Code of Canon Law: Baptism and sacramental life

17. Grace becomes visible in love

Read James 2:14–17. Ask students to identify the unmet material need in the passage before discussing the conclusion.

Connect the theological virtues to habits. Prudence helps discern a wise action; justice respects others’ due; fortitude gives courage; temperance orders desires.

Christians cooperate with grace. Avoid presenting deeds either as a way to purchase salvation or as irrelevant to faith.

The note emphasizes family, parish and community life: helping someone isolated, sharing resources, worshipping together, learning the faith and serving those in need.

Renewing baptismal promises at Easter involves rejecting sin and professing faith. Learning a patron saint’s story or remembering one’s baptismal date can support this identity.

Reference-only teacher-note connections retained: Mark 16:16 (faith and Baptism, in Mark’s longer ending); John 17:21 (prayer for Christian unity). The longer ending is received as canonical while textual notes distinguish it from the earliest manuscripts. Neither is an additional recorded reading.

18. Sharing Christ’s mission

Read 1 Peter 2:9–10. Link identity to declaring God’s works and receiving mercy.

Priestly example: pray for someone and offer ordinary work to God. Prophetic example: speak truth with charity when a rumour harms another person. Royal example: refuse to dominate and use influence to serve.

Christ’s kingship is expressed through self-giving service; it is not permission to control others. Optional connection: John 13:12–15.

This is a preview of Lesson 1.7, not a second full lesson on the three offices. Keep examples concrete and save extended explanation for the next lecture.

Evangelization includes sharing faith respectfully and supporting the Church’s mission; shared Baptism also calls Christians to dialogue and prayer for unity.

19. What would baptismal identity change?

Give pairs one minute to suggest an action and explain its reason. Do not require anyone to disclose baptismal status or a personal experience of bullying.

Possible links: adoption gives a secure identity beyond peer approval; membership in one body challenges exclusion; the Spirit helps courage; prophetic witness speaks truth; royal service uses influence for another’s good.

Every student deserves respect, baptized or not, because every person has human dignity. The Christian’s baptismal commitment adds a responsibility to live Christ’s love.

If the behaviour involves harassment or danger, include appropriate adult support. Forgiveness does not mean permitting harm.

Ask how prayer, a supportive community and moral habits could help the friend carry out the chosen action.

Catechism: the grace of Baptism

20. What is Baptism, and why does it matter?

Suggested assessment: a short paragraph or four brief answers, including one Scripture reference and one practical example.

Model understanding: Baptism is Christ’s sacrament of new birth by water and the Spirit. It forgives sin, gives grace and incorporation into the Church, and marks a person permanently. It joins us to Christ’s death and Resurrection.

It is necessary for those who know the Gospel and can seek it; the Church also teaches desire, blood and hope in God’s mercy for those without sacramental Baptism. Salvation always comes through Christ.

A strong application links a specific gift to a specific action: prayer, sacramental participation, conversion, service or witness. It should not imply that Baptism makes later faith and love unnecessary.

Optional personal reflection from the note: recall a fresh start and explain how identity can shape choices. Students may instead write about a hypothetical person.

Bridge: Christ saves in the Paschal Mystery (1.5); Baptism joins a person to that saving life (1.6); the shared mission of worship, witness and service follows (1.7). Every person deserves care, whether baptized or not.
