# Unit 1.3 — The Nature of God

Companion to **Nature_of_God_Lecture.html**, a 20-slide standalone lecture based on the supplied **Unit 1.3.docx**. Use an NRSV-CE Bible for the references below; the lecture intentionally provides prompts rather than printing passages.

## Intended understanding

Students should explain the Christian confession of one God in three Persons; distinguish transcendence and immanence; describe holiness, omnipresence, omniscience, omnipotence and personal relationship; and show how these claims belong together in God’s saving love. Assess understanding and reasoning, not personal religious assent.

## Teaching sequence and continuity

Allow about 60–70 minutes, adjusting discussion to the class. If dividing the lecture, pause after slide 10.

1. **Slides 1–7, about 20 minutes:** retrieve Lesson 1.1’s necessary/contingent distinction and Lesson 1.2’s primary/secondary causality. Move from existence and action to identity. Explain the Trinity, then critique the triangle analogy.
2. **Slides 8–10, about 12 minutes:** read Isaiah and Psalm 139. Ask students to connect holiness with mercy and to distinguish presence from knowledge.
3. **Slides 11–14, about 15 minutes:** consider prayer and freedom, read the Annunciation, discuss logical contradiction, and explain personal relationship using the Shepherd image.
4. **Slides 15–18, about 15 minutes:** read 1 John and John 3. Connect love with salvation, justice and a free response. Apply the teaching to a prayer scenario.
5. **Slides 19–20, about 5 minutes:** reflection and exit ticket. Collect a question for Lesson 1.4.

Lesson 1.1 raises philosophical questions about God’s existence; it does not by itself demonstrate the Trinity. Lesson 1.2 explains real creaturely action within dependence on God; this remains essential to prayer and freedom. Lesson 1.3 explains who Christians confess this God to be. Lessons 1.4 and 1.5 then develop the Incarnation and Paschal Mystery; Lesson 1.6 develops Baptism into this life. Later humanity, soul and moral-life lessons can retrieve divine love, dignity, freedom and charity.

## Activities and model responses

- **Slide 7, triangle:** a side is a part of a triangle, while each divine Person is fully God. A useful reminder of three and one must not become a complete account of the Trinity. Compare the limits of the artist/brush analogy from Lesson 1.2.
- **Slide 13, square circle:** a contradiction in the same respect is not a possible object. Being unable to lie expresses God’s perfection rather than a missing strength.
- **Slide 18, prayer:** knowledge, presence and love support honest trust; omnipotence is not a promise of every requested outcome. Combine prayer with practical help and responsible action.
- **Slide 19, reflection:** define one attribute, connect a Scripture reference and describe an action. A hypothetical or third-person response is acceptable; personal disclosure is optional.

## Teaching clarifications

The lecture preserves the note’s core content while qualifying several illustrations and generalizations:

- The Trinity is one indivisible divine nature in three distinct Persons, each fully divine. Neither triangle sides nor water changing states is a complete explanation. Do not divide the divine attributes or work between separate divine beings.
- God’s holiness does not mean an inability to be present to sinners. Isaiah’s vision itself joins holiness with purification and a call. The uncertain etymology of “holy” is not used.
- God’s presence is not a substance spread through space, nor is God identical with creation. Presence to all creatures is distinguished from indwelling through grace and the unique Eucharistic presence.
- Sheol in Psalm 139 is the realm of the dead, not simply a metaphor for sadness. The psalm’s poetry is not a spatial map of God.
- Knowing a choice and forcing it are different claims. This distinction helps frame foreknowledge and freedom without pretending to solve every question.
- Divine power is consistent with wisdom, goodness and truth. Confidence in prayer must not become a guarantee of health, success or a particular result; disappointment is not evidence of inadequate faith.
- Personal language is analogical. God is not biologically male; the Spirit is a Person, not an energy. “Personal God” does not add a fourth Person to the Trinity. The Son truly assumes human nature in the Incarnation.
- Creation is freely given, not needed to remedy divine loneliness. God’s attributes are not parts in competition. The Father’s sending and the Son’s self-gift share the one divine saving purpose; the Father did not become incarnate.
- Love is unearned but does not approve every action. Mercy, conversion, justice and protection from harm belong together. Do not diagnose the eternal fate or culpability of particular people.
