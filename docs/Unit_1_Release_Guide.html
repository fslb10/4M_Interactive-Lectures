One unit, a connected teaching path

The four movements form one arc: 1.1–1.3 distinguish arguments about existence and causes from revelation of God’s identity; 1.4–1.7 move from the Incarnation to Christ’s saving work, Baptism and shared mission; 1.8–1.9 ground care in inherent dignity and the unity of body and soul; 1.10–1.12 connect faith and reason, moral truth and a formed conscience. Each short has a spoken retrieval, application and exit/bridge; full versions place some transitions in notes and the shared unit map.

Core vocabulary was checked across both versions and the shared unit reference. Primary and secondary causes are distinct explanatory orders; Person and nature are not interchangeable; grace is gift with real cooperation; dignity belongs to all and is distinct from baptismal adoption; immortality is not the whole hope of resurrection; reason is broader than science; natural law is not instinct; conscience is a fallible judgment of reason; ethics evaluates reasons and rules. The six-step decision process is consistently applied in 1.10–1.12 with age-appropriate wording.

Assessment should match the selected version’s taught core. Ask for explanation, evidence and a reasoned action, not private disclosures or religious assent. Full-only historical detail, optional readings and extension cases should not be assessed after the short alone. All seven recorded anchors remain in both versions, including complete combined readings and skipped-verse boundaries.

Full 1.12 retains the approved practical revision: conscience/morality/ethics, Plato, Aristotle, Aquinas, Kant and Levinas, with extra Aquinas and Catholic formation. Levinas is Jewish; comparisons with Scripture do not recast him as a Catholic theologian. Short 1.12 derives from that version.

The historical short 1.1 note that said 1.2–1.4 were absent has been corrected as a release-status clarification. Its teaching script, timing, audio, captions and visible slides are unchanged. Earlier batch reports remain historical records; this release guide and release QA give the current state.

Full 1.12 has a flexible approved discussion plan; allow 60–70 minutes as a release planning estimate. Other full lessons use their guide ranges. Short timings include complete audio and teaching; 1.10 and 1.11 deliberately exceed ten minutes. All estimates are planning aids, not measured classroom durations.

1.1What makes an explanation convincing?What can reason, creation and human experience tell us about God?If God is the source of existence, how can nature and human choices be real causes?
1.2In 1.1 we asked why anything exists. Does dependence on God make created causes less real?If God sustains creation, how can nature and human choices be real causes?If God sustains creation without replacing its causes, who is this God?
1.3Existence and sustaining action lead to a new question: what can Christians say about God’s identity?Who is the God Christians understand as Creator and Saviour?If God is personal love, how is that love revealed in a human life?
1.4In 1.3 God’s love was central. How could that love enter human history?Why does it matter that Jesus is fully divine and fully human?We have explained who Jesus is. What does he accomplish for humanity?
1.5In 1.4 we explained who Jesus is. How can his death bring life?How does Christ’s saving work bring humanity into new life?How does Christ’s saving work become part of a person’s life?
1.6Christ dies and rises in 1.5. How can another person participate in that saving life?How does Baptism unite Christians to Christ and shape their lives?What does belonging to Christ call a Christian to do?
1.7Baptism gives belonging and a calling. What does sharing Christ’s mission look like?How does Christ’s identity become a mission Christians share?Why does every person deserve our service, including someone who is not baptized?
1.8In 1.7 service followed from belonging to Christ. Why does every person deserve that service?Why does every human person have dignity, and what follows from it?If the whole person has dignity, how do body, soul and eternal destiny belong together?
1.9Human dignity belongs to the whole person. How do body and soul belong together?How do body, soul and eternal destiny belong to one human person?How can we examine claims that involve science, philosophy and revelation?
1.10Return to 1.1: what is observed, what is inferred, and what is received through revelation?How do faith and reason work together in seeking truth and making choices?How does seeking truth help us recognize moral goods and duties?
1.11Dignity gives a reason to care; faith and reason help us seek truth. How do we judge actions?How do Catholics understand moral truth and put it into practice?How does a person form a conscience and judge a particular action responsibly?
1.12In 1.11 we studied moral truth. How do dignity and grace shape our personal judgment?How do we form a conscience and justify a responsible decision?Carry forward: how should a formed conscience guide our responsibilities in a wider community?
