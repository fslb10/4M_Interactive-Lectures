# Unit 1.7 — Jesus: Priest, Prophet and King

This 20-slide lecture follows the supplied **Unit 1.7.docx, “Jesus – Prophet, King, Priest.”** It develops the threefold mission introduced in Lesson 1.6. The central understanding is that Jesus reveals the Father, reconciles humanity to God and reigns through self-giving love; Baptism gives Christians a share in his mission through worship, truthful witness and service.

Allow approximately **50–55 minutes**, depending on discussion: 8 minutes for identity and the bridge, 12 for priesthood, 10 for kingship, 10 for prophecy and 10–15 for application. Select optional passages only where they help the class.

Slide 18 uses a service-club exclusion scenario to connect all three offices. Slide 19 consolidates the note’s deeper reflection and personal action plan. Both have revealable model responses. Students may use hypothetical examples without disclosing religious identity or personal experiences.

Slide 20 assesses four things: accurate explanation of the roles, a Scripture-supported salvation connection, an explanation of baptismal participation, and a justified student example. Model responses are in the notes. Assess understanding rather than a student’s profession of faith.

## Teaching distinctions

- Jesus’ two natures concern who he is; Priest, Prophet and King describe his mission. Neither makes him several persons.
- The Mass makes Christ’s unique sacrifice present sacramentally; Jesus does not die again. See [CCC 1362–1368](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_two/section_two/chapter_one/article_3/v_the_sacramental_sacrifice_thanksgiving_memorial_presence.index.html).
- Baptismal participation depends on grace and does not replace Christ’s saving work or confer ordained ministry. See [CCC 783–786](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_three/article_9/paragraph_2_the_church_-_people_of_god,_body_of_christ,_temple_of_the_holy_spirit.html) and [CCC 897–913](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2A.HTM).
- Christ’s kingdom is not derived from worldly domination, yet it has consequences for justice and relationships. Prophecy includes truthful witness in the present, not only prediction.
- The Spirit’s guidance is not a guarantee that every individual opinion is infallible. Christian witness requires learning and discernment.
