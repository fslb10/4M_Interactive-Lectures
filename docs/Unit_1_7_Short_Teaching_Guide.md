## Essential teaching clarifications

Priest, Prophet and King are aspects of the one Christ, not separate persons. His self-offering is unique; the Eucharist makes it sacramentally present without another death. Common and ministerial priesthood differ; Baptism does not confer ordination. A living sacrifice does not mean seeking pain or remaining in abuse. Prophet means witness to divine truth, not merely prediction; the Son reveals the Father as a distinct Person. The Spirit’s guidance does not make personal opinions infallible. Christ’s Kingdom has social consequences and cannot be equated with a party or country. Royal service includes self-mastery, protection and justice. Every person has dignity, regardless of baptismal status. The maintained sequence is Priest, Prophet, King, while the teacher note orders Priest, King, Prophet; every section is retained.

### Slide 2 · Luke 4:16–21

**Context**

- Early in his ministry, Jesus returns to Nazareth and reads in the synagogue on the Sabbath.
- He reads from Isaiah about the Spirit’s anointing, good news and liberation, then addresses those present.
- The excerpt ends with his announcement of fulfilment. The mixed response and rejection follow later in the chapter.

**Meaning**

- Christ and Messiah mean Anointed One: Jesus’ mission is empowered by the Spirit.
- Anointing connects baptismal identity with participation in Christ’s priestly, prophetic and royal mission.
- Attention to the poor and oppressed belongs to Christian witness, without reducing salvation to generic helpfulness.

Discuss: How does anointing connect identity and responsibility?
