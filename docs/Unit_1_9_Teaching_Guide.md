# Unit 1.9 — The Soul

20-slide lecture based on the supplied **Unit 1.9.docx: The Soul – The Immortal Essence of Human Identity**. Allow approximately 55–65 minutes, adjusting discussion time to the class. Use an NRSV-CE Bible for the read-aloud passages.

## Learning goals

Students explain the soul as the spiritual principle and form of the body; distinguish immortality from resurrection; connect Christian hope with Christ’s Paschal Mystery; and apply body–soul unity to dignity, conscience, choices and care for persons.

## Classroom activities and assessment

At slide 7, ask for one strength and one limitation of the hardware/software analogy. At slide 18, give pairs two minutes to address the student scenario before revealing the sample. Require integrity, bodily care and a specific constructive response. At slide 19, invite a short paragraph or letter: an experience, a question it raises, and one practice for the week. Personal bereavement, mental health details or declarations of faith are not required.

The exit ticket assesses: (1) body–soul unity, (2) immortality versus resurrection, (3) moral significance of eternal destiny, and (4) a practical whole-person response supported by Scripture. Model responses are in the presenter notes. Evaluate understanding and reasoning, not religious feeling.

## Teaching clarifications

- CCC **365** addresses form and unity; **366** addresses direct creation, immortality and reunion with the body. This corrects a citation in source section 1.
- The soul is created, not a pre-existing piece of God. Immortality does not mean having no beginning. “Spark” is metaphorical.
- Body and soul constitute one human nature. Death separates them; bodily resurrection is the final hope. The body is neither evil nor disposable.
- Intellect and will are not a dignity test. Bodily and neurological conditions matter; dignity persists when capacities cannot be exercised.
- Love, conscience and beauty invite philosophical reflection. They are not simple empirical proof of an immaterial soul. Scientific gaps do not establish theological conclusions, and materialist thinkers can value justice and love.
- Hebrews 4 does not teach two spiritual souls; CCC 367 clarifies soul/spirit language. Paul’s spiritual body is transformed bodily life, not a ghost.
- Purgatory is final purification of the saved, not a third final destination or a second chance after definitive rejection. Salvation depends on grace and calls for a free response.
- Distinguish particular judgment from final judgment. Entrust individuals to God’s mercy rather than claiming certainty about their eternal destiny.
- Grief and faith can coexist. Spiritual practices and appropriate medical, psychological and social support can coexist. Distress or addiction is not automatic evidence of spiritual failure.
