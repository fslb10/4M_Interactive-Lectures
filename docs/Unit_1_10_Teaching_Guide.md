# Unit 1.10 — Faith and Reason

20-slide lecture based on the supplied **Unit 1.10.docx**. Allow approximately 60–70 minutes, including seven read-aloud passages and discussion. The longer Genesis and Luke readings may be shared among readers. Use an NRSV-CE Bible; passages are not reproduced in full.

## Learning goals

Students distinguish natural reason from theological faith, explain their harmony in Catholic teaching, interpret biblical examples of faith, and apply informed judgment and Christian commitments to practical questions. Faith is presented as a gift of grace and a free response that bears fruit in love.

## Classroom use and assessment

Use slide 3 to collect everyday examples of reasoning. On slide 6, allow students to acknowledge the difficulty of Genesis 22 before discussing its context and ending. On slide 10, have students identify the precise claims behind an apparent conflict. On slide 12, test the strengths and limits of the “how/why” shorthand.

Slide 16 offers a four-step decision method: clarify facts, discern goods and duties, reflect with faith and counsel, then act and review. Slide 17 applies this to a hypothetical popular post; reveal the sample only after students identify the unsupported move from a scientific description to a moral conclusion. Slide 18 asks students to identify the Samaritan’s concrete actions. Slide 19 is the final reflection: question, investigation, faith commitment and next step. Personal declarations of belief are not required.

The slide 20 exit ticket assesses definitions, Scripture interpretation, natural law and practical application. Model responses are included in presenter notes. Assess clarity and reasoning, not religious feeling or agreement alone.

## Important teaching clarifications

- Faith is grace-enabled adherence to God and assent to revelation, not merely optimism, opinion or a feeling. It is free and cannot be coerced.
- Reason includes philosophical and moral inquiry, not only laboratory science or calculation. Emotion and empathy need not oppose reason. Human dignity does not depend on intellectual performance.
- Aquinas’s reasoning should not be reduced to “everything has a cause.” Distinguish dependent existence from an ultimate source and identify the premises of an argument.
- Genesis 22 is a difficult covenant narrative, not permission to harm others in response to a claimed divine command. Read through Isaac being spared. Christian typology does not make every detail identical to Christ’s sacrifice.
- John 20 records the invitation to touch the wounds, but does not explicitly say Thomas touched them. Distinguish the text from imaginative reconstruction.
- A revealed mystery exceeds exhaustive human comprehension; “mystery” is not an excuse for contradiction or refusing thought. Faith does not explain the specific reason for every tragedy.
- “Science asks how, faith asks why” is a useful opening but an inadequate rigid division. Science asks causal why-questions, and Christianity includes historical claims. Examine methods and actual claims.
- Natural law is moral understanding rooted in human nature and accessible to reason. It is not instinct, majority preference or copying animal behavior. People without religious faith can reason morally and act generously.
- The Good Samaritan connection is an application to practical mercy. The parable does not tell us the Samaritan’s inner philosophical reasoning, and reason alone is not synonymous with selfishness.
- The source’s end-of-life example is retained in presenter notes as a method illustration: clinical facts, dignity, compassion and qualified guidance. A general classroom slide cannot resolve an individual medical case.
- The source’s bracketed footnote numbers were not supplied with a bibliography. They are not reproduced as verified citations. Relevant primary Church sources are linked instead.
