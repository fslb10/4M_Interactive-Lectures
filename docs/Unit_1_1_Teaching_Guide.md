# Unit 1.1 — The Existence of God

20-slide lecture based on the supplied **Unit 1.1.docx: The Existence of God — Exploring Arguments, Evidence, and Perspectives**. Allow approximately 65–75 minutes, or split after slide 10. Seven Scripture pauses use an NRSV-CE Bible; the slides provide references and listening prompts rather than full passages.

## Learning goals

Students explain cosmological, teleological, moral and experiential approaches to God's existence; distinguish empirical findings from philosophical inferences; explain Catholic teaching on reason and revelation; and evaluate a premise respectfully. The lesson introduces the Creator as the foundation of the salvation story and points toward relationship with God through Christ.

## Classroom sequence and connections

Begin by collecting one question students have about God. On slide 2, distinguish an empirical question, a philosophical argument and an act of faith. Use slide 4 to establish the vocabulary of observation, premise, inference and conclusion. Those distinctions recur throughout the lecture.

For slides 5–10, ask students to explain what a scientific finding establishes before interpreting its philosophical significance. Slide 6 introduces contingency rather than presenting the Big Bang as a proof. On slide 8, ask what assumptions a probability claim requires. On slide 10, distinguish the origin of life from biological evolution and distinguish both from the theological claim of creation.

Slide 12 is a paired discussion before the response is revealed. A strong answer separates moral behavior, moral knowledge and moral grounding. Students should explain why the moral argument is not an accusation that non-believers cannot be good.

Slides 13–15 examine human experience. Students may discuss a hypothetical case, an artistic experience or the biblical narrative; personal disclosure is not required. Ask what an experience is, how it is interpreted and what reasons support the interpretation.

Slide 16 uses a school AI scenario to separate evidence about performance from judgments about dignity, fairness, privacy and responsible use. A strong response names both missing facts and moral principles. The activity does not assume that an actual product should be deployed.

Slide 18 moves from philosophical questions to specifically Christian revelation. Slide 19 is a roadmap when this is taught first, or retrieval when the class has already studied later lessons. Creation prepares the Paschal Mystery (1.5); conversion points toward Baptism and mission (1.6–1.7); consciousness and dignity prepare Humanity and The Soul (1.8–1.9); methods prepare Faith and Reason (1.10); moral awareness prepares moral truth and conscience (1.11–1.12). The broad preview of 1.2–1.4 does not prescribe their final titles or assume completed decks.

The exit ticket asks students to explain an argument, distinguish kinds of reasoning, state Catholic teaching and evaluate a claim. Model answers are in presenter notes. Assess reasoning and accurate understanding, not conformity of personal belief.

## Clarifications to the supplied note

- **Science and faith:** “How” versus “why” is a limited teaching analogy. Science answers causal why questions too. Ultimate explanation, moral obligation and theological meaning require additional forms of reasoning. Science has ethical norms, and scientists need not be religious to act responsibly.
- **Big Bang:** Describe a hot, dense early universe and expansion approximately 13.8 billion years ago. The model does not by itself establish an absolute beginning from nothing; a singularity is not an observed creation event. A scientific vacuum is not philosophical nothingness.
- **Aquinas:** His arguments include change, causation, contingency, degrees and directedness. Do not attribute every beginning-based formulation to him or reduce his arguments to a first instant in time. His account distinguishes creation's dependence on God from a philosophical demonstration of a temporal beginning.
- **Cause and contingency:** Avoid “everything has a cause except God.” The arguments concern caused, changing or dependent things. The conclusion of a necessary source requires its own reasoning and does not, by itself, establish the Trinity or every Christian doctrine.
- **Fine-tuning:** Some parameters affect conditions for stars, chemistry and life like ours. Do not imply that every small change to every parameter excludes all possible life. Probability claims depend on assumptions about allowed values, distributions and relationships. Design, deeper necessity, multiverse selection effects and unexplained facts must be discussed fairly, with their respective limitations.
- **DNA and evolution:** A code analogy is not proof that biological complexity cannot develop through natural processes. Natural selection is not simply random assembly. Creation and evolutionary explanation can be compatible. Current gaps in knowledge do not establish God by themselves.
- **Morality:** The claim that God grounds moral value is different from a claim about who can recognize duties or act well. Non-believers can act virtuously; non-theistic moral realists propose other accounts of objective morality. Conscience is fallible judgment requiring formation, not every feeling of guilt.
- **Catholic goodness:** God is understood as good, not as an arbitrary authority whose power alone makes any command right. Shared moral intuitions and cultural disagreement both need examination.
- **Consciousness:** Brain processes and subjective experience are related subjects of inquiry. An unresolved problem does not by itself demonstrate a soul or God. Catholic anthropology affirms the unity of body and soul, not a disposable body inhabited by a separate person.
- **Longing and experience:** Augustine and Lewis offer interpretations and arguments; desire alone does not guarantee an object. Powerful feelings or beneficial effects do not establish every religious interpretation. Lack of a dramatic experience is not a defect in faith.
- **Quotation and history:** Unresolved bracketed references and unverified quotations from the note are not reproduced as established sources. CCC 33's statement about origin in God concerns the spiritual soul; do not recast it as a standalone exact quotation about every inner voice. John Henry Newman is now styled Saint. Lemaître's example is used without an unverified interview quotation. Historical dialogue also includes conflict and error; it is not a story of effortless harmony.
- **Reason and revelation:** CCC 36 affirms reason's capacity to know God; CCC 37–38 recognizes obstacles. Do not imply that everyone who questions a classroom argument is irrational. Faith is a response to revelation supported by grace and exceeds assent to an abstract first cause.
