Words for thoughtful choices

How does the Christian understanding of God and the human person shape the way we live?

From learning to action

  1. Facts and uncertainty
  2. People and goods
  3. Chosen act and intention
  4. Relevant teaching
  5. Judgment and action
  6. Review and repair

Example: check why a student cannot join an activity, ask what would help, respect dignity, choose an honest practical response, and review its effects.

Reason

Using evidence, concepts and valid inference to seek truth.

Example: Ask which premise supports a conclusion.

Keep in mind: Reason is broader than laboratory science.

Faith

Free, grace-enabled trust in God and assent to what God reveals.

Example: Trust involves a response of the whole person.

Keep in mind: Faith does not forbid questions or replace evidence about ordinary facts.

Revelation

God’s self-disclosure in word and action, fulfilled in Christ.

Example: Christians read Scripture within the Church’s living Tradition.

Keep in mind: Not every personal feeling is divine revelation.

Primary causality

God gives and sustains the existence and action of created things.

Example: A plant depends on God for being.

Keep in mind: God is not one physical force competing with sunlight.

Secondary causality

Real action by created beings and natural processes.

Example: Water, sunlight and a gardener affect plant growth.

Keep in mind: Dependence on God does not make created action an illusion.

Person / nature

Person answers who; nature answers what kind of being.

Example: Trinity: three Persons, one divine nature. Christ: one Person, two natures.

Keep in mind: Three Persons are not three parts; two natures are not two Jesuses.

Incarnation

The eternal Son became fully human while remaining fully divine.

Example: Jesus has a real human body, mind and will.

Keep in mind: His humanity is not a disguise or a temporary costume.

Paschal Mystery

Christ’s Passion, Death, Resurrection and Ascension as one saving mystery.

Example: The Cross is understood in the light of Easter.

Keep in mind: The events belong together; mystery does not mean an unsolvable puzzle.

Passion

The real suffering Christ freely endured in love.

Example: Gethsemane reveals real anguish and willing obedience.

Keep in mind: Suffering is not good in itself; discipleship does not require accepting abuse.

Salvation / redemption

Salvation is rescue from sin and death into life with God; redemption names liberation through Christ.

Example: Forgiveness opens a renewed relationship and hope.

Keep in mind: Salvation is not earned by pain or flawless performance.

Grace

God’s free gift of help and participation in divine life.

Example: Christian action responds to God’s initiative.

Keep in mind: Grace is neither a wage nor a substitute for freedom.

Resurrection / immortality

Resurrection is the raising and transformation of the whole person; immortality means the soul does not cease to exist at death.

Example: Christian hope includes bodily resurrection with Christ.

Keep in mind: An immortal soul alone is not the whole Christian hope.

Sacrament

An effective sign of grace instituted by Christ and celebrated by the Church.

Example: Water and Trinitarian words belong to Baptism.

Keep in mind: A sacrament is more than a reminder or private symbol.

Dignity / baptismal adoption

Dignity is every person’s inherent worth; baptismal adoption is a further participation in God’s life through Christ.

Example: An unbaptized student has the same human dignity.

Keep in mind: Baptism does not make someone more human or more worthy of care.

Baptismal character

The permanent spiritual mark of belonging to Christ.

Example: Baptism is not repeated after a person sins.

Keep in mind: Permanent character does not remove the need for conversion and growth.

Priest, Prophet, King

Christ offers himself and intercedes, reveals the Father, and reigns through truth and service.

Example: The baptized participate through worship, witness and service.

Keep in mind: Baptismal participation is distinct from ordained ministry and Christ’s unique work.

Imago Dei

The human person is created in God’s image.

Example: Dignity grounds care for every person.

Keep in mind: Worth is not earned through ability, achievement, belief or usefulness.

Soul

The spiritual principle and form of the living human body.

Example: Body and soul form one human person.

Keep in mind: The soul is not transferable software in replaceable hardware.

Vocation

God’s call to love and holiness, lived through particular responsibilities and ways of life.

Example: Discern through gifts, needs, prayer and wise counsel.

Keep in mind: Vocation is broader than choosing a job.

Solidarity

Commitment to one another’s good.

Example: Stand with a student excluded from a trip.

Keep in mind: Concern must become shared responsibility.

Subsidiarity

Support decisions at the appropriate local level, with wider help when needed.

Example: Let affected students help shape an accessible plan.

Keep in mind: It does not mean leaving people without needed support.

Common good

Social conditions that help persons and groups flourish together.

Example: Design an activity everyone can access.

Keep in mind: It is not merely whatever benefits the majority.

Natural law

Moral goods and duties accessible to human reason, grounded in human nature.

Example: Reason can recognize that human life deserves protection.

Keep in mind: It is not a law of physics or simply following instincts.

Objective moral truth

Moral truth does not become true merely because someone prefers it.

Example: Humiliating a person is not justified by popularity.

Keep in mind: Objectivity does not remove the need to understand circumstances.

Act / intention / circumstances

What is chosen, why it is chosen, and the relevant context.

Example: Helping a friend is a good aim; lying to do it still needs moral judgment.

Keep in mind: A good goal does not justify an evil means.

Conscience

A judgment of reason about the moral quality of a concrete action.

Example: Judge whether giving a false account is right, with reasons.

Keep in mind: Conscience can err; a strong feeling is not proof.

Formation

Ongoing learning that helps conscience judge truthfully.

Example: Use reliable facts, Scripture, Church teaching, prayer, counsel and reflection.

Keep in mind: Sincerity does not remove the responsibility to keep learning.

Morality / ethics

Morality concerns right, wrong and the good life; ethics examines and applies moral reasons.

Example: A wallet case can be examined through duties, outcomes and virtues.

Keep in mind: The terms overlap; morality is not only private and ethics not only secular.

Virtue

A stable disposition to choose the good with practical wisdom.

Example: Repeated honest choices develop integrity.

Keep in mind: Virtue involves judgment and practice, not merely pleasant feelings.
